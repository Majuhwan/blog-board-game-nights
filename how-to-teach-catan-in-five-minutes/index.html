How to Teach Catan in Five Minutes

A short walkthrough of Catan's core rules, objective, and bonus tiles, based directly on the official CATAN GmbH rulebook.

Board Game NightsOct 9, 2026 · 4 min read

Catan has a reputation for being complicated, but its actual rulebook, published by CATAN GmbH, breaks the core game down into a short set of rules plus a reference section ("the Almanac") you only need to check when a specific situation comes up. If you're teaching new players, you don't need to read the whole rulebook aloud. You need the objective, the turn structure, and the two bonus tiles that confuse people most.

The objective in one sentence

The official rulebook states it directly: "The first player to reach 10 victory points (VPs) on their turn wins. Earn VPs by building. Collect and trade for the resources you need to build." That's the entire win condition. Everything else in the game exists to get players resources so they can build things, and building things is what earns points.

The turn structure

Each turn has two phases, in this order:

  1. Production phase: dice are rolled, and every player whose settlements or cities border the matching numbered terrain hexes collects resources.
  2. Action phase: the active player can trade, build, or play development cards.

New players often expect a fixed list of "actions per turn" like some other games use, but Catan's action phase has no fixed limit; you can build and trade multiple times in one action phase as long as you have the resources for it.

The two bonus tiles that confuse everyone the first time

Two special tiles award bonus victory points, and they move between players throughout the game, which is the part new players find hardest to track.

Largest Army: the rulebook states, "The first player to play 3 Knight cards receives this tile. If another player plays more Knight cards, they immediately receive this tile." This is worth 2 victory points for whoever is currently holding it. The key detail to explain clearly: the tile can change hands mid-game. If you have it with 3 knights played and another player reaches 4, they take it from you immediately, not at the end of the game.

Longest Road: the same mechanic applies to roads. "The first player to play 5 continuous roads receives this tile. If another player plays more, they immediately receive this tile." Also worth 2 victory points, and also transferable at any point during play, not locked in once awarded.

Explaining these two tiles as "temporary, not permanent" up front prevents the single most common rules confusion in a new group's first game: a player assuming they've locked in 2 points for the rest of the game when another player can still take the tile away from them on a later turn.

What you can skip for a first game

The rulebook organizes its content into three parts: the core Game Rules (4 pages), the Almanac (a longer reference for specific situations), and the Game Overview. For a first game with new players, you genuinely only need the Game Rules section. The Almanac exists specifically so you don't have to memorize every edge case in advance; you look it up only when a specific situation comes up that isn't covered by the basic rules (this is the rulebook's own stated structure: "if you have questions during the game, consult the Catan Almanac").

This matters for how you teach: don't try to pre-explain every possible card interaction or edge case before the first turn. Explain the objective, the two-phase turn structure, and the two bonus tiles, then let the group play and answer specific questions as they come up using the Almanac.

A five-minute teaching script

  1. "The goal is 10 victory points. You get points mainly by building settlements, cities, and roads." (30 seconds)
  2. "Each turn: roll dice, collect resources if your buildings touch the right terrain, then trade and build as much as you can afford." (60 seconds)
  3. "Two tiles give bonus points and can be stolen: Largest Army for most Knight cards played, Longest Road for the longest continuous road. Whoever currently has the most takes the tile, even mid-game." (90 seconds)
  4. "Everything else, we'll figure out as it comes up. There's a reference guide for anything unusual." (10 seconds)

That's under five minutes of talking before the first dice roll, with specific rule lookups deferred to when they're actually needed rather than front-loaded.

Key takeaways

  • The win condition is simple: first to 10 victory points on their own turn wins, earned mainly through building.
  • Each turn has exactly two phases: production (collect resources) and action (trade/build/play cards); there's no fixed action limit in the action phase.
  • Largest Army and Longest Road are each worth 2 points and can change hands immediately whenever another player surpasses the current holder's count.
  • Don't pre-teach every rule; the official rulebook itself is structured so that only the short core rules need to be read upfront, with edge cases looked up as they arise.

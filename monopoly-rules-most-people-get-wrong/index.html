Monopoly Rules Most People Get Wrong, According to Hasbro

Free Parking, auctions, rent, jail, and building rules from Hasbro's official Monopoly instructions, and why the house versions make games drag on.

Board Game NightsOct 11, 2026 · 4 min read

Monopoly has a reputation for games that never end. Hasbro's own current instructions suggest a reason: many families play with rules that are not in the box. The 2025 game guide even prints a warning card that reads "House rules could be making your Monopoly game longer!"

Below are the rules people most often change or forget, taken from Hasbro's current classic edition (product G0009, for 2 to 6 players, ages 8 and up) and checked against the older 2004 and 2007 instructions. If your family prefers its own version, that is your call, but it helps to know what the official game actually says.

1. Free Parking pays nothing

This is the big one. Many groups pile taxes and fines in the middle of the board and hand the pot to whoever lands on Free Parking. The official rules say the opposite. The current guide states: "You don't get money for landing on Free Parking. It makes the game longer!" and adds, "Never put cash in the middle of the board."

The older instructions say the same thing in more formal words: a player landing there "does not receive any money, property or reward of any kind." Taxes go to the Bank, and the Bank keeps them.

Why it matters: a Free Parking jackpot injects cash into the game, which keeps nearly bankrupt players alive and stretches every game.

2. Unbought property goes to auction, every time

If you land on an unowned street, railroad, or utility and do not want to buy it, the Banker must auction it. The current rules spell out how:

  • Anyone can bid, including the player who just declined it.
  • Bidding starts at $10, and bids can rise by as little as $1.
  • Players do not have to bid in turn order.
  • The highest bidder pays the Bank. If nobody bids, nobody pays and the deed stays with the Bank.

The guide repeats it on a reminder card: "Always auction when someone doesn't want to buy the property they've landed on." Skipping auctions is one of the main reasons properties sit unsold for an hour.

3. The owner has to ask for rent

Rent is not automatic. The current guide says that if the owner "doesn't ask for rent before the next player rolls the dice, you don't have to pay!" It also says there is no such thing as rent immunity, so deals like "you never charge me and I never charge you" are not allowed.

4. You can collect rent while in Jail

Going to Jail does not freeze your business. While in Jail you can still collect rent, bid in auctions, buy Houses and Hotels, mortgage, and trade.

There are three official ways out:

  1. Pay $50 at the start of your next turn, then roll and move.
  2. Use a Get Out of Jail Free card, including one bought from another player.
  3. Roll doubles on one of your next three turns. If you have not rolled doubles by the third turn, you pay $50 and move with that last roll.

Rolling doubles three times in a row on a normal turn also sends you straight to Jail, and you do not collect $200 for passing GO on the way.

5. Houses must be built evenly

Once you own a whole color set, you can buy Houses, and the current guide notes you do not have to wait for your own turn. But you must build evenly: no second House on any street until each street in the set has one, and no more than 4 Houses per street. A Hotel replaces 4 Houses, and each street can hold only 1 Hotel. You also cannot build on a street if any street in its color set is mortgaged.

If several players want the last available House or Hotel, the Banker auctions it, again starting at $10.

6. Players never lend each other money

The only lender in Monopoly is the Bank, through mortgages. The current guide says plainly: "Never loan money to other players." The older rules agree: "No player may borrow from or lend money to another player."

You can, however, buy, sell, or trade properties and Get Out of Jail Free cards with other players at any time, for whatever price you agree on, as long as no buildings are standing on that color set.

7. Mortgages have a cost to undo

To mortgage a property, first sell any buildings in its color set back to the Bank at half price, then flip the deed facedown and take the mortgage value. No rent is collected on a mortgaged property. In the current edition, you repay using the unmortgage cost printed on the Title Deed card. The older edition described the same idea as the mortgage value plus 10 percent interest.

Quick rent examples from the official rates

Utilities and railroads follow fixed rules in the current guide:

Situation Official rule Example
Land on a utility, owner has 1 4 times your dice roll Roll of 8 pays $32
Land on a utility, owner has 2 10 times your dice roll Roll of 8 pays $80
Land on a railroad $25, $50, $100, or $200 for 1 to 4 railroads owned Owner with 3 railroads collects $100

How the game ends

If you owe more than you can raise by selling buildings and mortgaging, you are bankrupt and out. The last player with money wins. Playing with auctions on and Free Parking empty is the simplest way to reach that point sooner.

Key takeaways

  • Free Parking pays nothing in the official rules; taxes and fines go to the Bank.
  • Any property a player declines to buy must be auctioned, starting at $10.
  • Owners must ask for rent before the next player rolls, and players never lend each other money.
  • Jail does not stop you from collecting rent, trading, or building.
  • Hasbro's current instructions warn directly that house rules can make the game longer.
